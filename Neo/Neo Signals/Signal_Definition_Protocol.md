# Signal Definition Protocol

**Purpose**: To systematically derive the **Signal Bias** of any NeOgham glyph or inscription based purely on its **Geometric Structure**, avoiding semantic or inconsistent interpretations.

**Target Audience**: Human Designers and AI Reasoning Agents.

---

## The Core Axiom
> **Structure = Bias.**
> Do not ask what a glyph "means." Ask what its physical geometry *makes easier or harder* for a relational system to do.

---

## The 7-Step Derivation Algorithm

### Step 0: Lock Constraints
Before analyzing, explicitly forbid semantic shortcuts.
-   **FORBIDDEN**: "Tree meanings" (Birch, Oak, etc.).
-   **FORBIDDEN**: Mythological associations (Celtic gods, etc.).
-   **FORBIDDEN**: Linguistic approximations ("This sounds like X").
-   **REQUIRED**: Derivations must rely *only* on structural properties that cannot be removed without destroying the glyph.

### Step 1: Structural Analysis
List the objective physical properties of the glyph.
-   **Orientation**: (Vertical Left, Vertical Right, Cross, Diagonal)
-   **Magnitude**: (Mark Count 1-5)
-   **Symmetry**: (Asymmetric, Bilateral, Rotational)
-   **Occlusion**: (Visible from one face? Both? Requires movement?)
-   **Coupling**: (Does it connect face A to B?)

### Step 2: Relational Primitive (RP) Mapping
For each of the six Relational Primitives, determining the bias exerted by the structural properties identified in Step 1.

| Primitive | Question to Ask | Bias Type |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ontology** | Does the structure encourage atomic singleness or composite complexity? | discrete vs. composite |
| **Dynamics** | Does the magnitude suggest a small nudge or a massive forcing function? | modulation vs. driving force |
| **Geometry** | What movement or position does the glyph demand of the observer? | directional vs. omni-directional |
| **Constraint** | Is the structure fragile (easy to break) or reinforced (redundant)? | fragile vs. rigid |
| **Epistemic** | Is the information fully visible instantly, or occluded by angles? | legible vs. ambiguous |
| **Meta** | Does the structure invert, map, or preserve relationships? | component vs. transformer |

### Step 3: Fingerprint Assembly (Synthesis)
Combine the RP biases into a coherent "Personality" or **Signal Fingerprint**.
*   *Example: "Biases atomic identity, high legibility, but low dynamical influence."*

### Step 4: Field Dynamics (Intuition)
Describe how this fingerprint affects the continuous **Signal Field**.
-   **Perturbation**: Sharp spike? Gentle wave? Deep well?
-   **Gradient**: Steep (forcing change) or shallow (allowing drift)?
-   **Basin**: Does it trap state (stable) or repel it (unstable)?

### Step 5: Closure Analysis (GCO)
Predict how the **Global Closure Operator** will react to this signal.
-   **Triggering**: Does this glyph alone force a decision? (Rarely for low magnitude).
-   **Stability**: If GCO runs, does this glyph survive or collapse?
-   **Context**: Does it need support from other glyphs to hold closure?

### Step 6: Sanity Check (Cross-Domain)
Validate the derived bias by mapping it to unrelated domains to ensuring the logic holds without semantics.
-   **Biology**: (e.g., binding site, promoter, inhibitor)
-   **Computation**: (e.g., bit flag, logic gate, buffer)
-   **Social**: (e.g., handshake, shout, whisper)
*If the structural bias remains consistent across these metaphors, it is valid.*

---

## Example Execution (Condensed)

**Glyph**: *Beith (1 Mark, Right Face)*
1.  **Structure**: Single, Asymmetric, Unilateral.
2.  **RP Map**:
    -   *Ontology*: Atomic (Simplicity).
    -   *Epistemic*: High Legibility (No occlusion).
    -   *Dynamics*: Weak (Low magnitude).
3.  **Fingerprint**: "Weak but clear atomic bias."
4.  **Field**: Small, sharp perturbation. Seed crystal.
5.  **GCO**: Unlikely to close alone; stabilizes easily in groups.
6.  **Check**: Biology = Binding site (exists or doesn't). Comp = Boolean Flag. Valid.
